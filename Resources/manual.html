The idea

Hawthorne works like a studio tape machine, not a DAW. There is one reel on the machine at a time. Every track runs in step on that reel from start to end. Recording goes onto the tape at the playhead and replaces what was there, exactly as on real tape. There are no clips, no playlists and no undo — you commit, as you would on a 24-track.

What you get in return is speed and focus: arm, roll, play. Around that core Hawthorne adds what a real studio has — tape stocks and machine models, varispeed, an autolocator, a master 2-track, timecode, a click, paperwork — and modern conveniences such as latency compensation and network audio.

First setup

  1. Open SETTINGS (top right). Choose your audio interface (ASIO for low latency), the sample rate, and turn on the inputs and outputs you'll use.
  2. Open LIBRARY and click NEW SESSION. Give it a name, choose the number of tracks, tape speed, tape stock and machine. A first reel is made for you.
  3. Open ROUTING. Patch an input to each track you'll record, and pick which tracks feed the master mix and which outputs they go to.
  4. For overdubs that line up perfectly, measure the round trip in ROUTING (see Latency).
The device's sample rate follows the reel: loading a 96 kHz reel switches the interface to 96 kHz.

Sessions and reels

A session holds the reels for one job (an album, a single, a client). A reel is one tape: its track count, speed, sample rate and tape stock are fixed when it's made, like a real reel. The library shows each session's reels on a shelf as tape boxes.

  • IMPORT RECORDINGS makes a new reel from existing recordings (stems or multitrack files). Pick the files, the session, the tape speed, the tape and the machine: the files are copied onto the new reel, one per track (stereo files onto two tracks, or mixed to mono), printed through the tape and machine you chose. The originals are only read, never changed. Choose Clean tape or no machine to bring them in bit-for-bit.
  • NEW REEL adds a reel to the selected session (defaults follow its last reel).
  • COLOUR sets a session's box colour; a reel can have its own.
  • The tape box shows the reel's details: speed, machine, tape, length, masters and what's on it.

Recording and punching

  1. Arm the tracks with their ARM buttons. Armed tracks hear their input when stopped or recording and the tape when playing back — tape-machine "auto input".
  2. Press REC (or R). Every armed track records from the playhead. The take is logged on the session sheet automatically.
  3. To punch in on a part, play and press REC at the spot; press it again to punch out. Punches crossfade with what was on the tape over 10 ms, so there are no clicks.
Recording replaces what was on the tape. That's tape. Use a fresh reel or another track when you want to keep a take.

Each pass is recorded sample-aligned with what's already on the tape: Hawthorne takes the interface's round trip (and any tape processing) off automatically.

Autolocator, auto punch and cycle

The autolocator (in the sidebar) holds two points, IN and OUT.

KeyDoes
IN / OUTClick to set the point at the playhead. Shift-click to go there.
AUTOAuto punch. With AUTO lit, REC spools back to the pre-roll, plays, and punches in at IN and out at OUT — on the exact sample, with crossfades — then stops after the post-roll. Press REC again to cancel. AUTO flashes while a punch is running.
REHRehearse. With AUTO, the armed tracks switch to their input between IN and OUT so the player can practise the punch, but nothing is recorded.
LOOPCycle. Plain playback loops from IN to OUT. With AUTO, the whole punch — pre-roll, punch, post-roll — repeats; each time round records over the last try (keep going until it's right, then stop). With tape wind physics on, the transport spools back to the start of the loop each time and rolls again, like a real autolocator (with CUE you hear it rewind); with it off the loop is seamless.

Pre-roll and post-roll are set per reel in SETTINGS → Transport.

Punches are sample-accurate at normal speed. At varispeed they land within a block and cut without a crossfade.

Markers

Drop markers with MARK or M. The marker list in the sidebar shows each one in min:sec (and bars.beats when the reel has a tempo). Click to go there; double-click to rename. , and . jump to the previous / next marker. Markers also set export ranges for masters.

Speed and varispeed

The deck runs at 7½, 15 or 30 ips. A reel recorded at 15 ips played at 30 runs twice as fast and an octave up, exactly like tape. VARI switches varispeed on; drag the knob (shift = fine) for ±50 %, or use −ST / +ST to step in semitones and 0 to reset. Percent and semitones show separately — double-click either to type an exact value.

Speed changes glide like a real motor. Recording at varispeed works: everything is resampled onto the tape so it plays back in tune at normal speed. Varispeed is locked while recording.

Spooling (wind) and CUE

With tape wind physics on (SETTINGS → Transport), locating to a marker, RTZ or the autolocator spools the tape like a real transport: it speeds up to fast wind (360 ips), brakes, overshoots a touch and creeps back onto the exact spot. STOP while spooling brakes; PLAY brakes and plays from where it stops. Turn it off for instant locates.

CUE is lifter defeat: the tape is held against the heads while it spools, so you hear the "chatter" — the music sped up into chirps — and can find a song by ear. Like a real machine, each track's repro head hears it, so it shows on the track meters and goes through the mix, the master meters and the track outputs. It's never recorded.

Tape stock

Each reel is made on a tape stock, chosen when the reel is created and fixed for its life. The tape is printed: what you record goes through the tape's record side (EQ, saturation, modulation noise) into the files, so it's part of the recording, just like real tape. Playback adds the tape's head bump, high-frequency loss and hiss live.

StockSound

High output (GP9-type)Clean, tight, most headroom; glue without obvious distortion.

EMI 60s (Emitape-type)The Beatles' Abbey Road tape: sweet early break-up, soft top, audible hiss.

Slower tape speeds give a bigger low bump, a darker top and more hiss. Hit the tape harder (more level) for more saturation.

Machines

MACHINE → Machines — or click the machine's badge on the deck or on the master meters — opens the machine room: a card for every machine with how it looks, its story and its sound (and a link to photos of the real thing). Click one to put the reel on it. It chooses the multitrack and the 2-track the reel plays on, and the deck, keys, counter and meters take on that machine's look. Unlike the tape, you can change them any time: the record side is printed when you record, the playback side follows the machine, so switching changes how existing tape sounds back. Each also has its own wow and flutter.

Noise reduction (Dolby A, Dolby SR, dbx)

Each reel can use a noise-reduction system, chosen when it's made (New Reel, New Session, Import recordings) and changeable from MACHINE → Noise reduction. Like the real racks, it encodes what you record (printed into the tape files) and decodes on playback, after the tape and its hiss — so the programme comes back as it went in and the hiss is pushed down.

  • Dolby A (1966): four bands, about 10 dB less hiss (15 dB at the top). The studio standard.
  • Dolby SR (1986): five bands, about 20 dB less hiss.
  • dbx Type I: wideband 2:1 compression and emphasis, about 30 dB less hiss — but it can breathe and pump on sparse material.

With noise reduction on, every track gets an NR strip under its meter: the system's badge, a lamp (green while encoding a recording, amber while decoding playback) and a four-way switch:

  • NR normal: encode on record, decode on playback.
  • ENC encode only: records encoded, plays back undecoded — the bright, breathy "Dolby trick" on vocals.
  • DEC decode only: records plain, decodes on playback — dull and expanded; also the setting for a track that arrived already encoded.
  • OUT bypass: neither.

The switch is also in a track's right-click menu, and MACHINE → Noise reduction sets all tracks at once. Switching a track after recording changes how it plays back (it's what's on the tape that counts). Imports are encoded and exports decoded following each track's switch. Varispeed, tape saturation and flipping the reel make the decoder mistrack a little, as on the real thing.

On the master: each master can have its own noise reduction too (None, Dolby A, Dolby SR or dbx), chosen when you make it and changeable from the master menu. Its badge shows beside the 2-track's. Master exports are decoded. Half-inch at 30 ips was often run with none.

Alignment (calibration)

MACHINE → Alignment opens the alignment panel, as a tech would line the machine up for each reel:

  • Operating level: 0 (185 nWb/m), +3 (260), +6 (370) or +9 (520). Hawthorne's 0 VU stays at -18 dBFS; a hotter level drives the tape harder — less hiss, but it saturates sooner. Each tape is made for one (456-type +6, GP9-type +9, 250-type and Emitape 0...), and new reels start there.
  • Record bias (overbias at 10 kHz, -1 to +4 dB): over-biasing is darker and smoother with a firmer low end; under-biasing is brighter, grittier and noisier. Double-click the knob for the tape's recommended bias.
  • Line-up oscillator: a 100 Hz, 1 kHz or 10 kHz tone at 0 VU into every track. The meters show it back off the repro head, so you can watch 10 kHz drop as you over-bias and the level compress as you go hotter.
  • Record tones: 1 kHz, 10 kHz and 100 Hz, 30 s each, at the head of the reel on every track (over anything there), with markers and a line on the take sheet.

The alignment is saved with the reel and fills in its Alignment field. Level and bias are printed into what you record from then on; hiss follows the current level.

Masters

A master is a stereo mix recorded onto the reel, sample-aligned with its tracks — the 2-track. In the sidebar:

  • Master menu: choose, create, rename and delete masters, pick the 2-track machine, and export stereo WAVs (the whole reel or marker to marker).
  • New master asks for its tape: mastering stocks (480/499-type, SM900-type) are only for the 2-track; Emitape and Clean work too.
  • ARM (master) records the mix along with the tracks; TAPE lets you hear the master tape instead of the live mix.

Reverse tape (flip the reel)

MACHINE → Flip the reel over turns the tape over: everything on it plays backwards. Record onto it (a guitar solo, cymbals, a vocal) and turn it back — the new part plays backwards against the song, the classic reverse-tape effect. Markers, takes and the autolocator flip with it. Optionally swap the track order too, as on a real machine where track 1 then plays on the last track. The deck header shows FLIPPED while it's over.

Import and export

  • Import (right-click a track's meter, or MACHINE menu): put a WAV, AIFF, FLAC, OGG or MP3 onto a track from the start or the playhead. Stereo files go onto two tracks or mix to mono. It's converted to the reel's sample rate and can be printed through the tape and machine. It records over that track, like any recording, and is logged.
  • Export: one track or all tracks (stems) as 24-bit WAVs the length of the reel, either as printed on the tape or with the machine's playback sound.
  • Masters export from the master menu.

Routing

The routing matrix patches the studio: for each track its input (one dot per row), whether it feeds the master mix, and its direct outputs; for the master its outputs and returns. Extra rows send the CLICK and LTC timecode to outputs. Network senders appear as extra input columns (NET 1, NET 2 ...).

Latency and outboard

Overdubs line up when Hawthorne knows the round trip — the time from the tape to your ears and back in through the interface. The driver reports a figure; for perfection, measure it in ROUTING: patch a cable from an output to an input, choose them, and click MEASURE. Hawthorne sends a click and times it. That figure is then taken off every recording.

Outboard on a track works the same: whatever comes back in on the track's input lands in time as long as the round trip is measured through the same converters.

Tempo and click

The tempo box in the deck: drag the BPM, double-click to type, click the time signature to change it, TAP to tap it in. TEMPO in the header has the details: bar 1 position, the click on/off and level. With a tempo, the counter and markers can show bars.beats.

The click is monitor only — it is never written to tape. Send it to outputs with ROUTING → CLICK, and/or tick In master outs. It follows the tape, so varispeed changes its tempo and pitch.

Timecode and sync

TIMECODE sets the frame rate and the timecode at the start of the tape.

  • Out: LTC on any output (ROUTING → LTC OUT) and MTC to a MIDI port — e.g. for Reaper to follow Hawthorne.
  • In / chase: click SYNC by the counter to choose INTERNAL or EXTERNAL. External chases LTC on an input or MTC from a MIDI port: Hawthorne locates, rolls when the timecode does, and stays in sync to well under a millisecond (it holds steady while recording).

THOREAU, the drum machine, syncs to Hawthorne over the network with tempo and position.

Hawthorne Link (network audio)

Record audio sent from other PCs on your network. Run Hawthorne Link (the sender app) on the other PC and choose what to send; it appears in NETWORK in Hawthorne. Connect it, and its channels appear as NET inputs in the routing. Hawthorne sizes the network buffer itself to avoid dropouts; set MIN BUFFER for lower latency on a good network. Senders on other subnets can be added by IP address.

For no drift at all over long takes, clock both interfaces from the same word clock.

Hawthorne Route (other programs in and out of Hawthorne)

Hawthorne Route is a virtual ASIO device that other programs on this PC can play into — for example to record tracks out of Reaper onto tape.

  1. Install it once: SETTINGS → Hawthorne Route → INSTALL DRIVER (Windows asks for permission).
  2. In the other program, choose Hawthorne Route as its ASIO device. It's two-way:
    • Its outputs 1–96 arrive in Hawthorne as inputs ROUTE 1–96 (patch them to tracks, or to the master returns).
    • Its inputs 1–96 are Hawthorne's tracks — each one exactly what that track's meter shows (tape on playback, the input when armed).
    • Inputs 97–98 are Hawthorne's master L / R, and input 99 is LTC of the tape position while it rolls — record it, or feed it to the program's timecode sync.
  3. In Hawthorne's ROUTING, patch the ROUTE inputs to tracks, arm and record as usual.

Hawthorne's own audio device clocks the other program, so the two never drift apart, and the sample rate follows Hawthorne. The other program can't use another audio device at the same time (one ASIO device per program). If Hawthorne isn't running, the other program still runs, silently.

Updates: when an update brings a new driver, Hawthorne asks to update it at startup (or press UPDATE DRIVER in SETTINGS). Close the other program first, then re-open Hawthorne Route in it to see the new channels.

Session sheets and printing

SHEET opens the paperwork, typed on paper:

  • SESSION: artist, title, producer, engineer, tape op, assistant, studio, room, console, monitoring, job number and notes.
  • REEL: the tape box label — reel number, date, EQ, alignment, noise reduction, tones, times and notes.
  • TRACK SHEET: for every track its name, source, mic, mic position, preamp / gain, EQ, dynamics, outboard / FX and notes — a full recall.
  • TAKE LOG: every take, filled in automatically; add the song, status and comments.
  • PHOTOS: add photos of the setup (mic positions, desk, outboard settings); they're kept with the reel.

PRINT makes a printable page of everything (with the photos) and opens it in your browser, ready to print or save as PDF.

Safety and recovery

  • Recording streams to disk continuously; if the disk falls behind, timing is kept and the gap recorded as silence, and you're told.
  • If the PC crashes or loses power mid-take, the next time the reel opens Hawthorne repairs the files and keeps everything recorded up to that moment.
  • If the audio device disappears while recording, the take is stopped and kept.
  • Hawthorne won't record with less than 500 MB of free disk space.
  • Deleted masters and photos go to the Recycle Bin.

Updates

When a new version is out, an UPDATE button appears in the header. Click it: Hawthorne downloads the installer, closes, installs and restarts. It won't update while recording.
